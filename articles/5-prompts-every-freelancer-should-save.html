5 prompts every freelancer should save

Freelancers repeat the same writing tasks every week: proposals, follow-ups, scope notes, tricky client messages. Save these five prompts in a notes app or text expander and reuse them. Replace the [brackets] each time, and always read the output before sending.

1. The proposal first draft

I'm a freelance [your service]. A potential client sent this brief: [paste].
Draft a short proposal (under 300 words) with: a one-line summary of their goal in
their words, my proposed approach in 3 steps, what's included and not included,
timeline, and a clear next step. Friendly, confident, no hype.
Leave [PRICE] as a placeholder.

Why it works: clients want to feel understood first. Restating their goal does that. You set the price yourself; never let AI guess your rates.

2. The scope clarifier

Here is a client request: [paste]. List the questions I should ask before quoting,
grouped into: deliverables, timeline, approvals/revisions, assets they'll provide,
and budget. Then flag anything vague that could cause scope creep.

Asking good questions up front is the cheapest way to avoid unpaid extra work later.

3. The polite follow-up

Write a short follow-up email to a client who hasn't replied in [X days] about
[topic]. Tone: warm, not pushy. Remind them of the one decision I need from them,
offer an easy option (e.g. a 10-minute call), and keep it under 90 words.

Keep a second version for overdue invoices: same structure, firmer tone, with the invoice number, amount and due date stated plainly.

4. The difficult message softener

I need to tell a client: [the hard thing, e.g. the deadline will slip by 3 days
because their assets arrived late / this request is outside scope and costs extra].
Write a message that is honest, takes responsibility for my part only, explains the
impact, and gives two clear options. No over-apologising. Under 150 words.

Write your raw, frustrated version first and let AI clean it up. You get to vent privately and send something professional.

5. The feedback-to-task list converter

Here is client feedback from email/call notes: [paste]. Turn it into a numbered
task list. For each item note: the exact change, which file/section it affects, and
whether it's within the agreed revisions or a new request. List anything unclear as
questions to send back.

This stops feedback from getting lost in long email threads, and it helps you spot out-of-scope requests before you quietly absorb them.

How to get better results from all five

  • Add context once: keep a short paragraph about your business (service, typical clients, tone) and paste it at the top of any prompt.
  • Protect client data: remove names, phone numbers and confidential details before pasting into any AI tool, and check your client contracts on confidentiality.
  • Edit, then send: the prompt saves you the blank page, not the responsibility. Read it as the client will.
  • Improve the prompts: when an output needs the same fix twice, add that instruction to the saved prompt.

Five prompts, saved once, can save you a lot of small writing time every week. More importantly, they help you communicate more clearly and consistently, which is what clients actually remember.
