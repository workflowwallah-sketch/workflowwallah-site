A 30-minute weekly newsletter template

Most newsletters die because each issue feels like starting over. A fixed template removes that. Here's a structure you can fill in about 30 minutes a week, plus how to collect material so you're never staring at an empty page.

The template

Subject: [One clear benefit or question, under 50 characters]

Hi [first name],

1. THE ONE THING (150–250 words)
   One idea, tip or workflow I tested this week. What I did, what happened, what you can try.

2. QUICK WINS (3 bullets)
   - A tool, setting or shortcut worth knowing
   - A prompt or template you can copy
   - A link worth your time, with one line on why

3. FROM MY DESK (2–3 sentences)
   A small honest update: what I'm working on, what didn't work.

4. ONE QUESTION
   Ask readers to reply with something specific.

– [Your name]

The 30-minute schedule

  • Minutes 0–5: open your notes file (see below) and pick the strongest idea for "The One Thing".
  • Minutes 5–17: write that section. If you use AI, give it your rough notes and ask for a tidy draft, then edit it in your own voice.
  • Minutes 17–23: fill in Quick Wins from your saved links and prompts.
  • Minutes 23–27: write the personal update and the question.
  • Minutes 27–30: write the subject line, send a test to yourself, check links on your phone, schedule.

Collect all week, write once

The real secret is a single running notes file (a phone note works). Whenever you learn something, solve a client problem or find a good link, drop a one-line note in it. By the end of the week you'll have more material than you need. Writing becomes choosing, not inventing.

Subject line tips

  • Be specific: "A prompt that cut my invoice emails to 2 minutes" beats "Weekly update #12".
  • Write three options and pick the one you'd actually open.
  • Avoid clickbait you can't deliver on; it costs trust.

Picking a platform

An example "One Thing" outline

Say you spent the week trying AI to reply to client emails faster. Your section might go: the problem (replies took 20 minutes each), what you tried (a saved prompt plus your own edit), what happened (faster replies, but one draft was too formal and you rewrote it), and the takeaway (use AI for the structure, keep the greeting and sign-off personal). Four short paragraphs, honest about what didn't work. Readers trust that more than a polished success story, and it's quicker to write because you're simply reporting what you did.

Keep yourself honest

  • Same day, same time each week. Consistency matters more than length.
  • Reply to every reader response early on; those conversations become future issues.
  • If you skip a week, don't apologise at length. Just send the next good issue.

Thirty minutes a week is about two hours a month. That's a small, sustainable investment, and after a few months you'll have a real archive of useful work and a list of people who chose to hear from you.
